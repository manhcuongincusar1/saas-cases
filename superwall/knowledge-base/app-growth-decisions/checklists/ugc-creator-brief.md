# UGC Creator Brief

**Run this when:** you are about to pay a creator for the first time, or you are
about to scale from a handful of creators to a roster.

**Two documents in one.** Part A is the brief you send the creator. Part B is the
deal you sign. Most founders write A and skip B, then discover there was no floor
under what they paid for.

---

## Part A — The brief you send

Copy this, fill the blanks, send it. Keep it to one page.

### The product moment

> **App:** ______________
> **What it does, in one sentence a stranger understands:** ______________
> **The single moment on screen that makes someone want it:** ______________

**The product must appear.** Every video shows the app. No cheap view-bait, even
when the bait would win more views — a video that goes viral without the product
is a video you paid for and cannot use.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s9qQPM92JNs&t=1005s)

**The product moment lands inside 15 seconds.** Past that it reads as an ad and
viewers leave. Seamless beats polished.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1336s)

### The structure

Hook → demo → app name, in that order. The name comes last, and quietly.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7eaHcznLZU&t=424s)

**Do not say the app name out loud.** Saying it flips the video from a story into
an ad. Let the comments ask what the app is.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I10xtUiXfeY&t=131s)

### The format

> **Reference video the creator should study:** ______________
> **What we are changing about it (aim for 10–20%):** ______________

Do not commission an original format. Find what is already working in your
category and remix it 10–20%. Founders lose weeks trying to invent a format when
the winning one is already on the For You page.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=432s)

To find it: scroll your niche for an hour a day. It is unglamorous and it is the
job. One founder found his format that way and remade the same video **400 times**.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=258s)

### Deliverables

> **Videos per month:** ______  **Story posts:** ______  **Link in bio:** Y / N
> **Deadline:** ______  **Raw files delivered:** Y / N

## Part B — The deal

### Pick a structure

| Structure | Pay | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Retainer** | Flat monthly | Testing a new creator |
| **Retainer + CPM** | Base + per 1,000 views | Scaling someone who works |
| **Minimum view floor** | Flat, with a guarantee | Creator's reach is unproven |
| **Equity** | Ownership, no cash | You have no money and they have the audience |

[All four explained →](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qYYeu0E-1SI&t=817s)

### The starting unit

**$200/month for four videos, four story posts and a link in bio**, from a creator
doing roughly 5,000 views per video — about 20,000 views for $200. That is the
cheapest honest test of whether a creator can sell your product.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOpYezjqU9c&t=931s)

Scaling up: **$500–1,000/month for 60 videos plus a per-1,000-view payment**, with
the per-video payout **capped at $1,000** and the CPM threshold starting at
**10,000 views**. The cap is what stops one lucky video from eating the budget.
[→ retainer + CPM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AtYlYzw40vo&t=593s) ·
[→ the cap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AtYlYzw40vo&t=642s)

### The clause most founders forget

> **Minimum view floor:** ______ views. If the video lands below it, the creator
> reposts or reshoots at no additional cost.

Without this you are buying a lottery ticket. With it you are buying views.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=575s)

### The onboarding bonus

Pay **$40 for the first video**, regardless of experience. Immediate reward beats
deferred reward; people who get paid fast make more videos.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=105s)

## Part C — Who to hire, and how many

### Read the comments, not the follower count

Followers do not matter at all. What matters is engagement rate and the views
per video. The working floor: **at least 5,000 views per video** — below that,
pass.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qYYeu0E-1SI&t=604s)

Some teams ignore followers entirely and hire for **on-camera presence**,
recruiting out of unrelated niches.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ca_VnMTosRo&t=423s)

### Keep the roster small

**Fewer than 20 creators posting consistently** beats a roster of thousands. One
company with 2,000 creators produced 500,000 views in a month — a number a single
good creator can beat.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1463s)

The sharpest version of this: build the equivalent of **the Navy SEALs, not the
Navy** — 10 to 12 part-time creators who genuinely understand the product.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=806s)

### Split the two jobs

A **creator manager** handles logistics. A **content coach** gives creative
feedback. They are different skills and the programs that last have both.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=564s)

Get a manager as early as you can afford one. Creators are hard to manage, and
that is the least-discussed fact about UGC.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=552s)

## Part D — Judging the work

### The one metric

**Percentage of viewers who watch past three seconds.** Target **75–80%**. Hitting
that consistently means the format works and virality is a matter of volume.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1624s)

### The second metric

**5% engagement rate.** A video that clears it earns ad spend behind it.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=77s)

### The trap

**On-target views beat raw views.** 10M views from your actual audience is worth
more than 40M scattered ones. One team's 40M-view video was their worst converter.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=500s)

### Where the money goes

Once something works: **20% of budget on making content, 80% on putting spend
behind the videos that already won.** Most founders invert this and wonder why
volume is not turning into revenue.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1562s)

### On AI-generated UGC

Across hundreds of campaigns it wins views and loses conversions. Use it to test
formats cheaply; do not build the program on it.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=821s)

If you use AI in production, the workflow that holds up is **AI does 95%, a human
does the last 5%**. Full automation does not convert.
[→](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=938s)
