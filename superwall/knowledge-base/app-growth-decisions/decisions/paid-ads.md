# When should I start paid ads, and how do I run them?

> **Conflicts of interest.** SideShift (Nick Lawson, Canyon, Drew Levin) is building a paid-ads product for organic winners — their thresholds sell that product. Marcus Burke and Evo are paid consultants/agencies; Driss (Methods) and Noise sell creator distribution as an *alternative* to ads; host Joseph Choi runs Consumer Club and now works at Superwall. Ad costs decay fast — every number below is date-stamped; prefer the newest.

## Inputs to collect first
- Monthly revenue and how much comes from organic; is organic flat for several weeks?
- Revenue per install (RPI) and cost per trial you can afford; trial→paid and renewal by cohort (see [paywall-pricing](paywall-pricing.md)).
- Business model: subscription ARPU vs social/ad-supported low ARPU.
- Library of organic winners (views vs the creator's normal, engagement rate, comments asking "what app?") and whether you own ad/usage rights.
- Category policy risk (health/peptides, dating/relationship advice, "grow your followers" tools draw rejections or bans).
- Audience age and country mix; tracking in place (Meta pixel/SDK events, MMP, country+device breakdowns).
- Cash runway: can you run at break-even for 4–8 weeks?

## Default answer
Start paid **after** two things are true: monetization works on organic traffic (RPI around $3+, trial→paid near 30%+) and you have organic videos that already convert. Begin on **Meta** at roughly **$100/day for two weeks**, running proven organic winners as **partnership/spark-style ads from the creator's handle**, re-cut so the viewer knows an App Store page is coming. Test creatives in volume (20–40 at small budgets; expect about 1 in 85 to carry spend), read results by country and device, optimize toward revenue rather than cheap trials, and scale only while ROAS stays positive. Paid margins are thin (10–30%) and creatives fatigue, so keep organic running as the creative lab.

## Decision rules
1. **If you can't make money from organic installs yet → don't buy installs.** Target RPI ≥ ~$3 (or cost per trial $10–15) before Meta. ### Worked example: estimating RPI before you have long cohorts
RPI = install→paid × net price × average paid periods per payer. Example: $7.99/week, 15% store fee → ~$6.79 net; 2.5% install→paid; payers stay 8 weeks on average → 0.025 × 6.79 × 8 ≈ **$1.36** — below the ~$3 bar, so fix conversion/retention first. Use real cohort revenue ÷ installs (60–90-day-old cohorts) as soon as you have it; the corpus has no retention curve for weekly subscribers, so the "periods per payer" guess is the user's own data, not a benchmark. Subtract per-user generation cost (image/video AI) during trials.

## When the default flips
- **Paid first**: high-ARPU or business-utility apps ("you spend 50, you make 100" — Alex on a business app, [00:38:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=2284s)), studios with ad machines (Social: 4,000 AI ads/month "profitably" for 2+ years, [00:01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=90s)), or founders with budget who want a fast conversion read (Yannis).
- **Never paid**: social/low-ARPU apps past cold start (Nomad Table), novelty apps whose ticket is too low (Yannis has seen people "spending way too much money on ads" with a low ticket — [00:21:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1315s)).
- **Statics can win** despite Marcus's warning that app-promotion campaigns deliver statics poorly ([00:19:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GUkXzJ2S6Z8&t=1173s)): Pep AI's best ad is a cluttered square static at <$2 CPI ([00:47:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOpYezjqU9c&t=2833s)).
- **Boosting organic doesn't always work**: Coconote put money behind its 18M-view video and "haven't really found a ton of success" ([00:09:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=573s)).

## Evidence against / where operators disagree
- **Organic-first vs paid-first.** SideShift, Sway, Pep, Bloom, Halo: organic first. Alex, Social, Welltory, Marcus: paid-native and profitable. The split tracks skill set and ARPU, not right/wrong.
- **UGC/organic vs paid economics.** Driss (*sells UGC*): UGC "four times higher converting, 10 to 20 times cheaper" than any paid source ([00:08:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=494s)); Noise calls Meta ads "super expensive" (*sells the alternative*, [00:07:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aNVPMvd8WU&t=442s)). Daniel (Cal AI): "if you can get paid ads to work, it's the best option" ([00:47:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=2876s)).
- **AI-made ads.** Social and Marcus: AI ads work at scale. Nick Lawson: AI UGC is "terrible for conversions" ([00:00:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=31s)) — he means organic UGC, not ad creative.
- **Talking-head.** Marcus: D2C talking-head "isn't the best format for apps"; Alex ([00:22:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=1323s)) and Evo ([00:17:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VGJWRjz6nq8&t=1069s)) win with a talking person over a green-screened demo or founder clip. Agreement point: the app must be visible.
- **Hit rates.** 1 in 85 (Alex, raw UGC volume) vs 20–30% profitable (Casper, curated premium creators). Different denominators.
- **Boost threshold.** Three SideShift people give three numbers (5%, 7.5–8%, 8–10%).

## Traps
- Buying traffic before the funnel monetizes (Steve's $1 RPI app).
- Optimizing for the cheapest trial — it buys the youngest, lowest-converting users ([Marcus 00:41:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GUkXzJ2S6Z8&t=2490s)).
- Copying a competitor's ad scripts instead of using your own proven organic ([Pep 00:06:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOpYezjqU9c&t=402s)).
- Judging on blended CPA and killing a winner that works in one country/device ([Yev 00:19:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXGmWdFLsuM&t=1163s)).
- Single-platform dependency: bans with no human support until you spend enough for a rep ([Alex 00:08:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=487s), [00:08:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=498s)); sensitive categories rejected on TikTok.
- Spreading budget across channels before finding one's ceiling ([Josh 00:49:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2966s)).
- Expecting a 2× ROAS winner to hold at 10× spend ([Yannis 00:18:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1100s)).
- Licensing creator content for only a few months, then losing the winner ([Casper 00:26:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I10xtUiXfeY&t=1583s)).
