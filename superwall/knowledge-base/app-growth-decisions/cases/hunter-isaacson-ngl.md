# Hunter Isaacson / NGL — platform shift + tight viral loop, monetised by a tiny paying minority

## Idea — how they found it
- **Platform shift:** Instagram opened story link stickers to all users at the end of October 2021 (before: 10k+ followers only); they started building on a Halloween Zoom call — [00:02:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=129s), [00:02:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=137s), [00:02:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=142s).
- Framework: take an existing internet behaviour (anonymous Q&A: Formspring, YOLO and others) and switch the distribution mechanism to the biggest graph — [00:11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=712s). Everything he has done well in social is built on top of existing networks — [00:07:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=458s).
- Assumed everyone would clone it; "no one cloned us until after we went viral" — [00:02:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=175s).

## Product — what they built and the key product choices
- Built in ~1 month; then 6 months with nobody using it — [00:02:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=166s). Fixes during that period: redesign, rename to NGL, domain ngl.link — [00:03:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=185s).
- Loop: two-step "copy link → share" with an auto-generated story asset; recipients tap the link inside Instagram, send a message, then see "get your own messages" — [00:09:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=557s), [00:09:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=593s). Selling the result ("get your own messages") converts far better than "download this app" — [00:10:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=640s).
- Onboarding 15–20 seconds; north-star metric = users posting their link and posting at least two replies — [00:10:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=655s), [00:11:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=673s).
- Kept the product almost unchanged since going viral; competitors died by adding feeds and complex profiles — [00:12:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=757s).
- Monetisation: weekly subscription, price set per country by GDP per capita ($1–7, "low impulse buy"); paid "hints" about the sender; only a very small % of users pay — [00:06:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=375s), [00:06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=390s), [00:06:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=394s).
- Retention via the graph: churned users re-download when friends keep posting links — "very high re-download rate" — [00:05:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=319s), [00:05:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=347s).
- Multi-step setup is fine if the user is already sold (seen friends get messages); same logic for share-sheet and Safari-extension apps — [00:25:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1517s), [00:26:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1619s).

## Marketing — channel, formats, creator/ads machine
- Seeding with tiny paid tests: "$100 here, $100 there" for Instagram stories and TikToks — [00:03:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=195s).
- ~4 months in, one paid influencer lit up a single school abroad; it spread locally but stalled because the country was mostly Android → built Android — [00:03:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=205s), [00:03:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=226s).
- May 2022, right after Android: one TikTok of a girl showing a provocative NGL message ("I was with your boyfriend last night") hit ~1M views and "took over the world over the next week"; 1,000 users in one day from it — [00:03:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=236s), [00:04:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=266s), [00:05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=300s).
- He puts invite/share screens in every product: a share is an endorsement — [00:27:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1637s).
- **$10k MRR → how to scale** (host reframes the budget to $20k/month): split it into many small UGC/ad experiments (a dozen creatives, not one), track cohorts per creative, and pair with product-led invites so the effective CPI drops — [00:27:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1657s), [00:27:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1674s), [00:28:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1700s), [00:28:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1710s).

## Portfolio — wins and failures
- First app (2017–2020): 2.5 years, paid dev agency, confusing UI, died in TestFlight — [00:14:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=843s), [00:14:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=886s).
- Zoom University (double-dating for college, seeded via meme pages/Facebook groups): 40,000 waitlist at 7:1 women:men, "maybe a couple hundred thousand downloads", top 10 Social — [00:15:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=914s), [00:16:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=968s).
- Wink (swipe to add on Snapchat): 70M downloads — [00:16:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=999s). A PvP mini-games app had ~1 hour time spent per user — [00:17:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1075s).
- PopStream (paid celebrity video meet-and-greets) killed: a top influencer posting a link cost ~$20k, economics failed — [00:20:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1244s).
- Bags (crypto): 1% fee on trades, majority passed to the inviter; launchpad 1% royalty ("$2M volume → $20k", illustrative) — [00:22:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1372s), [00:23:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1397s).
- On games: retention and paid-dominated acquisition make it "double hard"; small teams should not start there — [00:18:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=1131s).

## Lessons → decisions
- Look for platform shifts that open a new distribution surface, and port an old behaviour onto the biggest graph → [choose-idea](../decisions/choose-idea.md)
- A social loop where the output is shared on another network is a channel in itself; measure the north star (link posted + replies), not installs → [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md), [onboarding-retention](../decisions/onboarding-retention.md)
- Mass-market social can monetise with cheap, GDP-adjusted weekly subs on a tiny paying share — only at huge volume → [paywall-pricing](../decisions/paywall-pricing.md)
- Six flat months did not mean "kill": the fix was distribution (Android in the right geography + one TikTok), not features → [build-or-kill](../decisions/build-or-kill.md)
- Spend scaling budgets as many small creative tests + a referral mechanic, not one big bet → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md), [paid-ads](../decisions/paid-ads.md)

## Caveats
- Short (30 min) interview; revenue only as "multi-8-figure", no costs or margins stated. The summary line about "margins he actually kept" rests on the host's intro only.
- Survivorship: NGL rode a one-time Instagram policy change in late 2021; the window is closed. NGL-style anonymous apps carry teen-safety/regulatory risk (not discussed).
- Bags is a crypto trading product — revenue tied to on-chain volume spikes; not comparable to subscription apps.
- Hunter is a Consumer Club member; episode sponsored by Superwall — [00:09:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTree0unT_0&t=546s).
