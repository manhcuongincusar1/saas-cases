# Which app idea should I build, and how do I validate demand before building?

## Inputs to collect first
- **Goal**: cash-flow app ($10–200k/mo, sellable) or venture scale? Decides how narrow and how novel the idea must be.
- **Distribution asset**: Does the user (or a partner) post content, have an audience, or know a creator who would take equity? Can they film themselves daily? If not, is the problem typed into App Store search by name?
- **Validation budget**: $0 (founder posts), ~$1–5k (test ads, influencers, UGC trial), or more.
- **Build capability**: engineer, AI coding, no-code, or needs a technical partner.
- **Audience**: age (Gen Z vs 30–40+), country, which platform they live on, can they pay.
- **Personal link** to the problem or community (drives persistence and believable founder content).

## Default answer
Don't invent from a blank page. Take demand that is already proven (an app already making money, a content niche already going viral, a pain people publicly ask about in comments and subreddits) and move it into an audience or angle nobody serves well. Before writing much code, check two things. (1) The result can be shown in a few seconds of video, or people search the problem by name. (2) Strangers will pay: a manual service, a Stripe pre-order, or a test ad or influencer post that converts. Joining a waitlist does not count. For a first app, aim for a narrow cash-flow win. Novelty and moats matter later.

## Decision rules
1. **If you have no idea yet → copy a proven mechanic into an audience nobody serves.** GlamUp = Umax for women, checked the App Store, decided in a 10-minute call. PrayScreen = the 1sec screen-pause app rebuilt for Christians. Coconote = AI note-takers, which then targeted professionals, rebuilt for students. Pep AI = GLP-1 trackers widened to all peptides ("more to market"). Sunflower cloned a "quit X" app. Runafy = LiftOff's gym rank system rebuilt for running. **[strong]** — [GlamUp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bf12MNPO-Js&t=264s), [PrayScreen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=282s), [Coconote](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=1316s), [Pep AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOpYezjqU9c&t=237s), [Sunflower](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFfBuyKoaDM&t=2974s), [Runafy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=3264s)
2. **If choosing between ideas → require proof the money already exists nearby.** Caleb's rule: 1–3 apps in the space making ≥$100k/mo. A $5k/mo comparable is not enough, and VC-funded comparables (e.g. one that raised $24M) hide whether the economics work. Steve Young wants a competitor that earns money but has a weak onboarding and paywall. Daniel: a big established market means you test one variable (your novel solution), not two. Connor: check what earns money on the App Store, then cross it with TikTok trends. **[strong]** — [Caleb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=3271s), [Caleb VC comps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=3319s), [Steve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=724s), [Daniel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=2371s), [Connor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zpTXi8WxeM0&t=1422s)
3. **Filter by willingness to pay: painkiller over vitamin, high-monetizing categories.** Sway dropped a personal-finance idea ("hard to monetize") for dating. YourMove: if your landing page has to explain why the problem matters, it's a vitamin. Steve: meditation is a vitamin; green noise and migraine tracking are painkillers you can hard-paywall. Casper: "spend $X, get more than $X back" sells itself. Halo's founder wants health, wealth and relationships next because those retain and earn more per user. **[strong]** — [Sway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=152s), [YourMove](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1795s), [Steve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=683s), [Casper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I10xtUiXfeY&t=2095s), [Halo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PFfo4JySptw&t=3312s)
4. **No channel = no idea.** The idea must be either (a) instantly readable in a short video, or (b) typed into search by name. Daniel's three criteria: simple and easy to understand in marketing videos, a novel mechanic, a big market. Pingo's co-founder: "I don't want to build an app that's not going to go viral," so they asked which AI feature is inherently viral. Stronger designs features as screenshots: "those screenshots are what make us money." Search route: the paruresis app could not be marketed on TikTok, but most users and revenue came from App Store search. Steve finds the keyword first, then builds. If neither (a) nor (b) holds, drop the idea. **[strong]** — [Daniel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=2239s), [Pingo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s9qQPM92JNs&t=394s), [Stronger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=si3HzkBTPl4&t=1626s), [paruresis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=750s), [Steve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=485s)
5. **Find demand where it is already written in public**:
   - comment sections full of "how?": Raphael built Debloat AI after seeing ~100 such comments; "the comment section is a really good indicator of how strong a problem is"
   - subreddits where people post asking for help: Sway from the Hinge subreddit; the paruresis app from a Reddit community of thousands; Cardstock after finding no app on the store
   - sponsorship offers a creator turned down: Casper's $500/video offer became Payout; Connor asks creators which apps pitch them
   - content niches already viral: Daniel cites horoscopes→Astra and motivation content
   - cultural waves with an existing "cult": Dan Kwan
   - a new AI capability pointed at existing demand: Halo started from the Gemini image API; Coconote asks "what is possible today that wasn't 3–6 months ago"; Evan says stay on top of model releases

7. **Then get money, not emails.** Sway charged Instagram followers $15 for a manual profile review before the app existed.    - Social's studio runs standardized ads for apps that don't exist yet and compares them with its benchmark

8. **Size the niche: narrow enough to win, wide enough to matter.**
   - Canyon (SideShift): very narrow niches (knitting, ballet, wrestling/MMA) convert better and keep a winning format alive longer, but most top out around $10k/mo unless you pull in adjacent audiences (ballet → all dance).
   - The paruresis app converted at 30%+ but made only $5–10k lifetime, so it was abandoned. YourMove's first project had a total addressable market under $1M and failed.
   - Connor aims "right in the middle, then slightly more niche," for $30k to a couple hundred thousand per month. Josh (M&A): define the category narrowly enough to be #1, but keep it believable.

   **[strong]** — [Canyon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1360s), [Canyon ceiling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1421s), [paruresis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=774s), [YourMove](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2099s), [Connor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zpTXi8WxeM0&t=2109s), [Josh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=571s)
9. **Check that the niche has distribution to buy.**
   - Daniel: count how many creators the niche has and what they cost. Fitness has many at reasonable prices; beauty creators are priced up by makeup brands. Dating coaches won't promote an app that cannibalizes their own profile-review product.
   - Dawson: apps for 30–40-year-olds are underserved (travel, settlement claims) but need older creators and Facebook/Instagram.
   - Canyon: the "make money" niche (side hustles, settlement apps) is saturated, with more account suppression as of 2026-09.

   **[medium]** — [Daniel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=2586s), [Daniel conflict](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=853s), [Dawson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VGJWRjz6nq8&t=1365s), [Canyon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1302s)
10. **First app → pick something you can win at your experience level.** Lots (sold 4 apps): "get your first nut first… very hard to get there on your first try." Connor targets $30k–a few hundred k/mo apps "to train my skills." Matt: copying best practice gets you to $10k–100k MRR, and beyond that you need to be novel. Kobe: "this isn't probably going to be your first app." **[strong]** — [Lots](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1115s), [Connor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zpTXi8WxeM0&t=2118s), [Matt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ca_VnMTosRo&t=255s), [Kobe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFfBuyKoaDM&t=1349s)
11. **Avoid idea shapes operators say retain or monetize badly**:
    - an AI companion alone ("the whole thing is horrible": engagement, retention, nobody pays)
    - hype or entertainment image generation (Halo is moving to core problems)
    - games built only to look good in a TikTok (Raphael: "we never really cracked retention")
    - social networks, which need huge scale and have low revenue per user (Social avoids them; Nomad Table is bearish on paid ads for them)

    **[strong]** — [Kobe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFfBuyKoaDM&t=948s), [Halo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PFfo4JySptw&t=3312s), [Raphael](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIgn-1kbzZw&t=838s), [Social](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=356s), [Nomad Table](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2165s)
12. **If the founder has no pull toward the problem → weight that heavily.** Roger Chen killed the AI-story app Super Movie because he "did not feel that much passion" to keep tweaking details. Reed (Fitted) looks for founder-market fit in his creator partner. **[medium]** — [Roger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jZ1W85VdIBk&t=2319s), [Jack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=1192s), [Fitted](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9655E10fxsk&t=1579s)

## When the default flips
- **Venture-scale or long-term goal → novelty and moat matter more than arbitrage.** Matt says copying stops working past $100k MRR. Coconote: without a real moat (network effects, brand), you must keep producing "wow." Josh: nine-figure outcomes need a category you can own plus retained cohorts. Daniel still pairs novelty with a big existing market. — [Matt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ca_VnMTosRo&t=255s), [Coconote](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=2503s), [Josh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=854s)
- **You are the creator, or own an audience → build for that audience.** Validation shrinks to "will my followers pay." Casper's first idea came from a turned-down sponsorship and "just blew up." Raphael launched meme games from his own following. — [Casper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I10xtUiXfeY&t=393s), [Raphael](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIgn-1kbzZw&t=473s)
- **Private or embarrassing problems → skip content validation.** Validate through search volume and competition instead (paruresis; Steve's keyword-first). See [aso-search](aso-search.md).
- **A crowded "commodity" category can still work** if you add a visual or status mechanic. Stronger's mentors all said a workout tracker would be "a commodity"; their rank heat map became both product and marketing. Daniel: calorie tracking was an old space until photo → calories. — [Stronger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=si3HzkBTPl4&t=348s), [Daniel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=2239s)
- **You have budget but no content skill → validate with paid ads first.** Yannis: "do a bit of paid ads, and figure out if your product is good and converts." Organic suits impulse or novelty products and low budgets. — [Yannis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1271s)

## Evidence against / where operators disagree
- **Copy vs novel.** Lots, Connor and Caleb say copy into a new audience. Matt says copying caps out around $10–100k MRR. Coconote says the "wow" half-life is shrinking and copycats arrive within a week. The right answer depends on your goal (see [When the default flips](#when-the-default-flips)).
- **Is a waitlist evidence?** Dan (Massive, 2025-08): useless, ~0.5% converted. Caleb (2026-03) collected emails too, but the signal was the 90+ who paid, and he reused the list at launch with a half-price code. Count payments, not emails.
- **Narrow vs big market.** Canyon and Casper favour narrow niches (easier to ideate, better conversion; ceiling ~$10k/mo). Daniel favours big established markets for durable growth. Connor splits the difference. Josh (M&A) favours narrow positioning inside a large market.
- **Ride a wave vs build something durable.** Dan Kwan and Raphael ride memes and cultural moments. Raphael admits ~50 such games never retained, and Dan paused Shepherd's UGC to build social loops. Bloom's view, raised by the host and agreed by Jay: you may need only a marketing *angle* into a movement, not a new app — [Bloom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AtYlYzw40vo&t=1928s).
- **Which validation channel?** Daniel: influencers are "a great way of testing the idea really quickly" because you test only downloads and payment ([00:48:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=2909s)), though "not all ideas work with influencers." ## Traps
- **Views without intent.** Yannis's scratch-map video with a "post-breakup" overlay went viral with no downloads; "procrastinating for exams" moved App Store ranks. A viral validation video proves nothing unless it converts — [00:40:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2405s).
- **Too-small market.** YourMove's first project had a total addressable market under $1M. Paruresis converted brilliantly but stayed tiny — [YourMove](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2099s).
- **Building only for your own age group.** Raphael: few founders build for boomers or gardeners, yet a plant app makes "probably like 3 million a month" (his unverified estimate). Dawson: most vibe-coded apps chase Gen Z "get rich quick" — [Raphael](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIgn-1kbzZw&t=1585s), [Dawson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VGJWRjz6nq8&t=1310s).
- **A product for "anyone."** Canyon (2025-07): a product with no target niche produces bland content — [00:11:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=704s).
- **Judging the market from funded comparables.** Their spend hides weak unit economics (Caleb) — [00:55:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=3319s).
- **Category that fights your distribution.** Dating coaches won't promote an app that replaces their service (Sway).

## Gaps
- **Survivorship bias.** No guest describes ideas that passed these tests and still failed, so there is no base rate for any validation method.
- **No head-to-head comparison** of validation methods (pre-order vs fake ad vs influencer test). The payment thresholds above come from one operator each.
- **Market-size inputs** (Sensor Tower, keyword volume) are third-party estimates the corpus never checks.
- **Scope.** Almost all cases are iOS-first, US-focused, consumer subscription apps. Android-first, B2B, hardware and non-English markets are barely covered.
- **Decay.** AI-wrapper and trend windows close in weeks. Evidence runs 2025-04 to 2026-10; re-check anything trend-dependent.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Superwall hosts and sells paywalls. Joseph Choi sells Consumer Club. SideShift (Canyon, Nick) sells creator access. Steve Young sells ASO and app-buying services. Josh is an M&A buyer. Evan's speedrun advice points to a builder tool ("10x") he promotes.
