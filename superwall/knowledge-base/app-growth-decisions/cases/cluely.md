# Cluely / Methods — few creators, many formats, and framing as the growth lever

## Idea — how they found it
- Instinct: after leaving Cluely, "the one thing every company desperately needs is distribution" [04:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=240s); Driss went on sales calls with **no product** and signed contracts worth ~$1.2M annualized in the first week (Sept 2025) — [03:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=220s), [04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=264s), [04:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=280s).
- Methods: same service turned into a two-sided app. Cold-start fix: sign companies first, then pay creators to post — [12:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=758s).
- Origin of his skill: dropshipping fake AirPods Max in high school — tried 10+ products, barely broke even for ~1.5 months, then a recut of other people's unboxing videos + a ~$5 voiceover hit 200k views overnight — [17:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1032s), [17:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1071s), [17:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1077s), [18:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1108s). Minecraft Shorts channel before that: 6 months at ~zero, then 200k subs / 180M views in 9 months (sold) — [22:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1320s), [22:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1354s).

## Product — what they built and the key product choices
- **Framing beat features**: "Methods is the UGC marketplace app" → nobody joined; "the new way to make money online" → explosive growth, user count "doubling every two weeks", first search result for "make money online" — [09:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=555s), [09:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=568s), [09:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=570s). Same on the B2B side: "we'll run your UGC" sounds lame; "we'll make you money / conversions / viral" gets paid — [09:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=548s).
- **$40 first-video payment** regardless of experience, to beat the fear of flopping and the "quit after the second video gets 100 views" failure — [12:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=770s), [13:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=838s), [15:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=901s). (He also says "$30 to make a video" once — [12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=765s).) Then exact scripts, example videos, account warm-up, lighting guidance, AI feedback on every post — [52:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=3166s).
- Product philosophy: "people want to feel performative" — "I don't know a single person that has learned a language off of Duolingo", yet it has hundreds of millions of users; single-task standalone apps (calorie trackers, Bible apps) feel more trustworthy than asking ChatGPT — [34:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=2075s), [34:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=2082s). Hide the AI: outside tech, people say they avoid products that use AI — [46:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=2782s).

## Marketing — channel, formats, creator/ads machine
- **Cluely UGC**: with Ben, 0 → 100M views "in the first few weeks" at sub-$1 CPM — [06:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=364s) (intro narration says "2 weeks" — [00:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=14s)). First working format was a "Snapchat format"; pushed to other creators, then deliberately killed and replaced — [23:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1406s), [23:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1424s). Another team member then scaled it further — [23:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1430s).
- **Few creators**: "less than 20 consistently posting" — [24:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1463s). Counter-example: a company with 2,000 creators got 500k views in a month — [24:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1490s). Top 1% / 0.1% of creators ≈ 90% of views; scale content, not headcount — [25:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1501s).
- **Why UGC programs fail**: they find one format and stick with it instead of experimenting; managing creators at scale is hard — [23:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1414s).
- **Ideation**: most successful formats are "taken from other companies in similar niches or completely different" and adapted; "all new ideas are references of existing works" — [26:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1566s), [27:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1626s). Yet "you will not be extremely successful copying anyone else" (applies to strategy, not formats) — [38:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=2319s).
- **Viral vs converting**: content is either extremely viral or extremely converting; Cluely's cinematic Twitter launch videos were top-of-funnel for tech/investors, UGC drove users — [07:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=464s). Claim: for client companies UGC is "four times higher converting, 10 to 20 times cheaper" than any other paid UA source — [08:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=494s).
- **Decart (client)**: AI photo/video app, 2-person marketing team; 100M views in 2 weeks at sub-30-cent CPM, top 30 App Store, 400M+ lifetime — [04:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=298s), [05:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=302s), [05:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=305s).
- **Viral sense decays**: must be regained on every new platform/niche — [21:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=1278s). Founders: "how hard is it to record one 30-second video every day?" — don't hire for what you've never done — [52:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=3133s), [52:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=3155s).

## Lessons → decisions
- Start with a handful of creators who can repeatedly get views; scale content per creator before headcount → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)
- Keep testing new formats; kill a working format before it decays; borrow formats from other niches → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md), [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md)
- Pay a small guaranteed amount for the first post to get new creators past the first flops → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)
- Framing/positioning of the same product can be the growth unlock ("make money online" vs "UGC marketplace"); it also drove search rank → [choose-idea](../decisions/choose-idea.md), [aso-search](../decisions/aso-search.md)
- Viral launch videos ≠ users; UGC for conversion, cinematic content for awareness → [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md), [paid-ads](../decisions/paid-ads.md)
- Founder should make content personally before hiring for it → [build-or-kill](../decisions/build-or-kill.md), [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)

## Caveats
- **Conflict of interest**: Driss sells UGC services (Instinct) and a UGC platform (Methods); he pitches hq.methods.app in the episode [41:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=2497s). The 4x/10-20x UGC claim and "anyone can go viral" are vendor claims.
- He credits the Cluely result partly to Ben and the team; "100M in 2 weeks" is the narrator's framing, his own words are "first few weeks". No Cluely revenue, conversion or retention numbers given.
- "$1.2M ARR" = annualized signed contracts in week one, not collected revenue. The "$50k/month" dropshipping figure is read by the host from his website [16:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szgT-8gXd3s&t=980s), not stated by Driss.
- He states he ignores advice from others by design; most claims are single-operator (weak). Published 2026-03-01; hosted by Superwall; SpyTok sponsor read.
