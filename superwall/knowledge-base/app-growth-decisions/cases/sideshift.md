# SideShift — what a UGC marketplace sees across hundreds of campaigns (and sells)

## Idea — how they found it
- Began as a "mom-and-pop" marketplace for restaurants and bars hiring college students. A year before mid-2026 "our creators sucked" — [00:25:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1509s). To seed supply they physically visited 25 campuses for the first 10–20k users — [00:40:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=2451s).
- They ran the playbook on themselves: high-volume organic took them from ~10k to 150k active job seekers in 3–4 months for ~$10k total spend — [00:00:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=47s), [00:01:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=83s). Their own campaign used 5 creators and ~300 posts for ~16M (maybe ~20M) views, and the winning format took 2–3 weeks to find — [00:02:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=178s), [00:03:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=236s).
- Winning format: "denied from a job despite a master's degree". It was later remade as a talking head (9.6M views, 16% engagement) — [00:17:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1079s). After Nick cracked it, 6–7 of the next 15 creator videos hit 150–300k — [00:20:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1201s). A year on it is a good *warm-up* format but "doesn't convert super well" — [00:18:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1096s).

## Product — what they built and the key product choices
- They "mint" their own supply through a ~6-hour Duolingo-style creator course, and they front money that brands owe creators ("almost six figures a month") to keep supply loyal — [00:25:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1523s), [00:28:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1711s).
- Payment features: pay only for the best of 4 cross-posted platforms (Cluely runs this) — [00:40:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2402s). Sparked views are separated from organic views so creators are not paid view bonuses on paid reach — [00:29:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1786s).
- V3 (Sep 2026): run organic winners as Meta/TikTok ads from the platform and pay creators a % of ad spend or revenue. It adds attribution overlays (App Store, Stripe, RevenueCat), a "Sidekick" agent, a campaign-manager course and ~30 partner agencies — [00:37:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=2257s), [00:43:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=2631s), [00:46:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=2817s), [00:51:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=3094s).

## Marketing — the playbook they see working (dated per episode)
**Start (08/2026, Nick).** Search competitors on TikTok/IG and remix a proven format 10–20% — [00:07:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=432s). Or take a non-brand viral "problem" video and add a CTA — [00:17:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1036s). Month-1 budget is $2–4k: $600–800 per creator for 30–60 videos, as a base plus a $1–2 CPM, capped or uncapped — [00:04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=290s), [00:07:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=473s), [00:08:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=511s). In 07/2025 the advice was $15–20/video plus a base retainer plus bonuses — [00:14:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=853s).

**Content–market fit.** Nick: you have it when videos are consistently >10k views, "maybe five or six out of the entire campaign"; he calls a video viral at 100k+ — [00:09:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=578s), [00:10:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=605s). Canyon (2025): "sometimes it takes 300 posts", and ~2 weeks of organic is usually enough to judge an idea — [00:04:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=299s), [00:33:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=2031s). After fit, 90% of creators run the winner and 10% (1–2 creators) keep testing — [00:21:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1294s), [00:22:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1330s).

**Pay (06/2026, Drew).** The best programs pay $800/mo for 60 videos (2/day, cross-posted to 4 platforms) plus *non-stacked* view bonuses. New creators get $200–300 for the same package. Four months earlier "$400 base + $2 CPM" was the norm — [00:43:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2614s), [00:43:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2620s), [00:46:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2764s). Optimise for "free views" after the view window closes; above $30–50k/mo of spend these add up to "huge money" — [00:44:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2649s). Caps backfire: one creator was capped at ~$4k after an IG account did 180M views in 2 weeks (a 45M-view video). Uncapped he would have earned ~$75k, and the client lost him — [00:45:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2707s).

**Team (09/2026, Canyon).** In the discovery phase the key role is strategist/coach: find formats, review every video *before* it posts. If nobody holds that role, the founder does it. In the scale phase the key role becomes recruiter — [00:08:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=483s), [00:08:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=533s), [00:09:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=549s). First hire is a generalist manager on a 1-month pilot at $2–4k/mo (~$150 per creator) plus ~5% of all creator bonuses — [00:05:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=311s), [00:05:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=341s). Drew puts the manager:creator ratio at 1:10 (up to 15; under 5 wastes money) — [00:11:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=680s). Once sub-$4 CPMs are consistent, founders step back. Founders of the top-50 programs only do payouts and monthly check-ins — [00:12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=740s).

**Creator selection.** Prefer storytelling to camera, responsiveness and a "face card", and ignore past view counts. Creators newish to UGC are cheaper and more loyal. More than 5 concurrent campaigns is a red flag: they ghost after 1–2 weeks without a hit — [00:09:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=593s), [00:11:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=705s), [00:13:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=822s). Briefs should hold reference videos, hook ideas and CTA placement, not word-for-word scripts — [00:15:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=945s). Warm up new accounts for 3–5 days by "acting like a human in the niche" — [00:18:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=1102s).

**Platforms & niches (09/2026).** Over the prior 3–6 months, Instagram drove ~10–20% more aggregate views than TikTok at similar posting volume. Facebook Reels gets added via one-click cross-post. TikTok shadowbans "monetization" niches (crypto, side hustle, settlement apps, SideShift itself) — [00:19:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1171s), [00:19:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1198s), [00:20:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1239s). Ultra-narrow niches (knitting, ballet, wrestling/MMA) convert better and keep winning formats alive longer, but "comfortably scale up to… $10,000 a month". Reaching $100k–$1M/mo needs adjacent audiences (ballet → the whole dance community) — [00:22:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1346s), [00:23:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1421s), [00:27:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1622s). Earlier (06/2026) Drew said: apps where users earn money or get something back (Settlemate, Snag) do best organically, and AI products should "lean into the fact that people hate AI" (Suno) — [00:04:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=283s), [00:06:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=374s).

**International (09/2026).** A US creator costs ~$25/video; LATAM creators of similar quality cost ~$15. Use cheap markets as format-testing grounds, with 3–5 creators per market, one market at a time — [00:33:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=1987s), [00:35:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=2102s).

**Organic → paid. The three co-founders give different thresholds:**
- **Nick (08/2026):** ≥5% engagement earns ad spend. Start at ~$100/day *total* for 14 days. Some teams run $300/day, the biggest up to $100k/day — [00:28:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1729s), [00:31:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1897s), [00:30:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1837s).
- **Drew (06/2026):** engagement = (likes + comments + saves + shares) / views; 7.5–8% is good and >12% goes very viral — [00:18:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1104s). Spark when a video hits >~7.5–10% or 3–5× the account's average views (that also helps lift shadowbans). Rank candidates by comment sentiment, then engagement, then views. Run $50–100/day per video for a week and kill it below 5%. Don't spark while organic is still climbing — [00:30:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1808s), [00:35:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2155s), [00:36:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2214s), [00:37:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2239s). Meta Partnership ads convert better than Spark, even on small UGC accounts. Target audiences early on Meta; optimise for reach on TikTok — [00:38:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2335s), [00:39:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2345s).
- **Canyon (09/2026):** run as an ad when a video gets 5–10× the creator's normal views, 8–10%+ engagement and product questions in the comments. Organic flops can still win as paid — [00:50:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=3010s), [00:49:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuUJgmbQrVc&t=2992s).
- Enterprise clients split budgets ~20% creative/organic and ~80% performance — [00:26:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=1568s).

## Lessons → decisions
- Run UGC in two phases: discovery (strategist reviews every video) then scale (recruiter, 1:10 managers). Expect $5–8 CPM in month 1 → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)
- Use base + non-stacked view bonuses and never hard-cap a breakout creator; optimise for "free views" → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)
- Promote organic winners to Spark/Partnership ads on engagement + comment sentiment, and start small → [paid-ads](../decisions/paid-ads.md)
- An ultra-narrow niche gets you to ~$10k/mo fast; pick one with an adjacent audience if you want more → [choose-idea](../decisions/choose-idea.md)
- Instagram/FB Reels now out-deliver TikTok in aggregate; "earn money" apps face TikTok shadowbans → [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md)
- 2 weeks and ~30–50 videos is enough to read an idea. If views come but installs don't, question the product → [build-or-kill](../decisions/build-or-kill.md)
- Organic discovery can lift App Store page conversion sharply (Photogenic 5–10% → 50%+) → [aso-search](../decisions/aso-search.md)

## Caveats
- **Strong conflict of interest.** SideShift sells marketplace access, campaign managers and now an ads product. "UGC works", "AI UGC doesn't convert", "hire a campaign manager", "Spark through us" and "go international" all serve its sales. The host co-founded SpyTok, plugged in the 2025 episode — [00:43:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_a4ch3pk_U0&t=2620s). He also runs Consumer Club, and Superwall sponsors the show.
- **"AI UGC doesn't convert" is opinion, not shown data.** Nick says "I've not seen it convert" — [00:13:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=830s). The "conversion data across hundreds of campaigns" line is the video's narration — [00:00:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=40s). Counter-evidence: Social says all its paid ads have been fully AI for 2+ years — [00:01:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=87s).
- **The co-founders disagree.** Spark thresholds differ (5% vs 7.5–10% vs 8–10%). The host says a Spark ad can carry an install anchor to track CPI, and Drew refers to "direct conversion numbers" on Spark — [00:31:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1877s), [00:32:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1934s). Nick says Spark has no anchor and only CPM is measurable — [00:33:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2dVvzvwtBY&t=2035s).
- Client numbers are secondhand and mostly unnamed, from a platform showing its winners (survivorship). The 20/80 split describes enterprise marketing budgets, not an indie-app rule. Pay rates and CPMs decay fast: in 06/2026 Drew already called the structure from four months earlier outdated.
