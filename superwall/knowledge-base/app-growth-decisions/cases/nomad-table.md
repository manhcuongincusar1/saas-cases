# Nomad Table — founder finds the formats himself, then scales them through creators on pure-ish CPM; why a social app skips paid ads

## Idea — how they found it
- Personal problem from a 3-week solo trip (PTO from a big-tech job): loved hostel social life, hated sharing rooms with 8 people and unsocial hostels → "create that same social magic as a hostel without having to actually stay in a hostel" — [04:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=274s), [50:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=3020s).
- Quit about a week after returning; de-risked by savings and big-tech "rehire within a year" norm — [52:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=3156s). Worked in big tech 3 years; totally bootstrapped; some early months in the red, profitable now — [35:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2108s).

## Product — what they built and the key product choices
- Create an activity (drinks, sightseeing, padel) → nearby solo travelers are notified and join — [04:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=255s).
- **Cold start**: waitlist of ~12–15k before launch, only ~6k installed on day one — [47:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2844s), [47:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2852s). Worked because the app needs only "two people in one place"; hotspots like Barcelona had ~20 users — [47:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2859s), [47:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2874s). Recommends Andrew Chen's *The Cold Start Problem* — [47:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2832s). No location-by-location launch (dating-app playbook) because travelers leave each city within weeks — [45:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2748s).
- **Monetization**: subscriptions (weekly/monthly/annual, annual ~$69.99 per host, Jay confirms) — [07:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=442s). Social app = low ARPU by design; refuses in-app ads even though he estimates they could push revenue "easily over 100k a month" — [06:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=387s).

## Marketing — channel, formats, creator/ads machine
- **Founder first**: got the first ~50–100k downloads from his own content, then hired creators once confident formats repeat — [08:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=500s). Waited until he could go viral himself because paying creators for unvetted formats is "useless"; first creators hired in April — [53:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=3215s). Still the top-views creator in his network for "2–3 months" — [10:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=649s). "I only have my creators remake the ones that hit — all the flopping's done on my end" — [26:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1596s).
- **Early founder-led content** (face, story, public sign-up milestones) built an audience that "wanted me to win" and stuck through a rough v1 — [48:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2931s), [49:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2960s).
- **Winning format = text hook + immediate product demo.** First viral video (app still on pre-order): "I've been solo traveling for 2 years and now I find out about this 🤦" → phone on table → demo — [12:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=767s), [13:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=804s). Hook lifted from another niche ("I did not invent this one") — [13:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=820s). Specific details ("the most unsocial hostel in Thailand") make it believable — [15:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=918s).
- Hook gimmicks (drinking, tying a shoe) showed no real effect: "a good hook is a good hook" — [23:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1432s); a good text hook works across creators regardless of acting ability.
- **The view trap**: clickbait hostel hook ("this random girl in my hostel showed me this…") got 300k views but 174 likes and 8 comments — hook promises something gross, delivers an app — [17:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1050s), [18:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1114s). Also unscalable (required being in a hostel) — [20:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1233s).
- **Format-quality signal**: % viewers past 3 s; 75–80% consistently = good format that "is going to pretty much for sure go viral eventually"; average watch time ~6–10 s — [27:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1657s), [27:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1676s). Good format floors at ~4–5k views/post on TikTok; sub-50 = "cooked" — [29:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1741s), [29:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1756s). Counter-evidence he volunteers: a month of posts all at ~81% past 3 s with nothing going viral — [30:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1815s).
- **Hit rate**: ~1 viral video/month per account posting once a day (his and most creators') — [32:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1923s). A network spreads the luck; 60–70 creators × ~5k baseline views/day is already "a lot of downloads" — [32:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1949s). Viral videos keep earning for months, one for "a whole year" — [33:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1984s).
- **Creator ops**: 60–70 creators, managed alone in one big WhatsApp group + one-on-ones — [09:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=555s), [09:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=568s). Creators mostly stick to his formats, with some freedom to avoid boredom — [11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=712s).
- **Pay**: "a bit of a mix, but mostly all performance based on views" — [38:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2327s); $1–2 CPM — [40:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2445s); highest offer he has seen from other apps $3 — [42:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2525s). Creators accept CPM only once you can show the app goes viral (your own account: "I've done this format like eight times") — before that, pay retainers — [39:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2383s), [40:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2407s). No payout cap (though he says capping is probably wise) — [43:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2634s); pays views on old posts for ~3 months — [44:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2687s).
- **No paid ads**: "pretty bearish on paid ads for social apps" — low ARPU; paid only to break a cold start; a social app should grow by word of mouth — [36:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2165s), [36:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2194s). Contrast drawn with high-ARPU betting apps that must run ads — [37:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2223s).

## Lessons → decisions
- Founder posts until formats repeat, then hands only proven hooks to creators; proof of virality is what lets you move creators from retainer to CPM → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md), [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md)
- Judge formats on hook retention and engagement, not raw views; clickbait that doesn't pay off the hook is a view trap → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md), [build-or-kill](../decisions/build-or-kill.md)
- Low-ARPU social apps: avoid permanent paid acquisition; use paid at most to break a cold start; aim for word of mouth → [paid-ads](../decisions/paid-ads.md), [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md)
- Social cold start: waitlist + an app that works with 2 users per place + founder-led story content → [onboarding-retention](../decisions/onboarding-retention.md), [choose-idea](../decisions/choose-idea.md)
- Low price/ARPU is a deliberate trade for user base; no in-app ads → [paywall-pricing](../decisions/paywall-pricing.md)

## Caveats
- Self-reported, approximate ("I want to say"); no conversion rate, CAC, LTV or retention figures given. Word-of-mouth share is an onboarding-survey estimate.
- Narrator intro overstates: "60 creators, 44 million views a month" (44M was one month; Jay says 30–40M) [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=0s) and "virality becomes predictable and inevitable" [01:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=87s) — Jay himself calls virality "a bit of a lottery" [28:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1730s).
- Watch-time thresholds are from his own Instagram insights in one niche; he says "I'm not sure how that applies across the board" [29:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1756s).
