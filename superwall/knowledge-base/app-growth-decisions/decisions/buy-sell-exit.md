# Should I buy, sell or exit an app, and at what price?

## Inputs to collect first
- **Role:** seller (offer in hand? from whom?), buyer (cash, loan capacity, operator skill), or holder weighing both.
- **Revenue:** MRR/ARR, profit or EBITDA margin, months of revenue history, trend (growing / flat / past peak), seasonality.
- **Traffic mix:** % organic (search, word of mouth) vs paid vs creator/influencer-driven. Who owns that distribution: the app, the founder, or one influencer?
- **Retention and pricing:** year-1 resubscribe rate, retention curve of the top 20% most engaged users, weekly vs monthly/annual plans.
- **Goal:** cash flow or "life legacy"? Capital needed for the next project? Still enjoying it?
- **For buyers:** which specific lever you will pull (keywords, paywall, ads, localization) and whether you are actually good at it.

## Default answer
Small consumer apps trade at single-digit multiples of ARR, roughly **2–6x**. The top of that range needs growth, sticky revenue and strong margins. **Sellers:** decide cash flow vs legacy first. Sell from strength before the peak. Value certainty of closing over the headline price, because most deals die. Present the levers the buyer can pull that you haven't. **Buyers:** buy apps whose distribution is already built in (ideally organic search) and that are under-monetized, and only when you own the specific lever that fixes them. Avoid apps that depend on paid ads or on one influencer unless that is your skill. **Hold** while growth is fast, you have no better use for the time or capital, and you are enjoying it.

## Decision rules
1. **Decide what you're building for before you price anything:** "am I building this for cash flow or am I building this as my life legacy?" ([Lots 00:21:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1284s)). "Not everyone is chasing an exit… so many people are just chasing a really strong cash flow business" ([Josh, Blue Throne 00:31:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1919s)). **[medium]**
2. **Price range:** "2x to 6x… of ARR generally" ([Lots 00:21:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1279s)). Best case is ~6x revenue run rate, and only with a growth story plus 30–50% EBITDA ([Josh 00:06:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=387s)). Slower or less profitable apps get valued on the last 12 months ([00:06:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=406s)). Even a brand-new app got "5X, 4.5, 5X, I can't remember exactly" on estimated MRR ([Caleb 00:47:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=2829s)). **[strong]**
3. **Run-rate needs stable revenue.** Buyers extrapolate a quarter or six months, never one hot month, e.g. a fitness app's January ([Josh 00:07:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=472s)). Three months of history is "good", and buyers don't want one day at $100k MRR and the next at $20k ([Lots 00:22:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1368s)). Twelve months of growth is "way more convincing than 3", because three months "could really be distilled down to like three viral videos" ([Josh 00:44:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2694s)). **[medium]**
4. **What moves the multiple up** (buyer checklist; score yourself in the table):
   - sticky revenue. "Revenue multiples are really just present value math on LTV", measured by cohort retention ([Josh 00:14:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=889s)) and by the top-20%-engaged curve flattening ([00:15:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=927s))
   - organic share of installs ([00:20:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1239s))
   - premium pricing ([00:24:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1499s))
   - being #1 in a believably narrow category ([00:09:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=575s))
   - a repeatable machine: "I don't want to know the last video that went viral. I want to know the machine you built to replicate it" ([00:23:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1393s))

   Organic over paid: **[medium]**. Steve: an app that is fully paid "is a money eating machine" ([00:44:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2655s)). For the opposite case, see "Evidence against".
5. **Sell before you need to, and before the peak.** "The biggest predictor of your exit price… is your alternative to the exit" ([Josh 00:43:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2603s)). "Let the buyer pay for the peak that's coming in the future" ([00:46:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2764s)). Boost App Social "negotiated from a place of strength… we weren't in a rush" ([Alex 00:33:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=1984s)). **[medium]**
6. **Optimize for close certainty, not top price.** "Most deals fall through, like 90% plus. I would rather take like the third highest price if they can… close in the next four, five days" ([Lots 00:21:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1297s)). "Most of those conversations are not going to be a great use of time and energy" ([Coconote 00:35:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=2108s)). **[medium]**
7. **Sell the upside, not the effort.** Don't pull every lever yourself. Lots never internationalizes his apps so he can tell buyers "you haven't even tried this, this, this, and this" ([00:22:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1322s), [00:22:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1335s)). Ask each buyer what drove returns on their past deals (ads, pricing, features) and pitch the piece they are missing ([Josh 00:42:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2551s)). **[medium]**
8. **Match the buyer type** ([Josh 00:35:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2131s)) **[weak]** (one M&A operator; the acqui-hire branch also has Matt's case):
   - **Aggregators/platforms** (e.g. Blue Throne): can run the app without you.
   - **Strategics:** "generally willing to pay the highest multiples" because of synergies, e.g. Strava–Runna as top of the funnel ([00:35:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2151s)).
   - **Private equity:** cash flow + EBITDA, and "you as the founder have to stay on board and run the thing for 3 to 5 years" ([00:37:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2220s)).
   - **Acqui-hire/IP:** rare for consumer apps. Jenni AI's purchase of Matt's app "was essentially in the end an acqui-hire" ([00:44:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ca_VnMTosRo&t=2673s)).
   - Past your peak → PE. Peak still ahead → aggregator ([00:47:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2849s)).
9. **If distribution sits in one person (founder or influencer),** the deal has to lock that person in. Influencers often refuse earn-outs, so these apps are "tricky" to sell ([Josh 00:30:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1814s)). **[weak]**
10. **Run the process deliberately:**
    - Build relationships with target buyers 6–12 months ahead and read their last five deals ([Josh 00:38:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2286s)).
    - A lean team should use an investment bank or broker so the product doesn't stall. Coconote's banker ran it "from early conversations all the way through to the end of closing" ([00:35:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=2129s), [00:36:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=2189s)). Alex used a broker ([00:32:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=1950s)).
    - Offer a 1–2-month transition upfront ([Lots 00:23:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1407s)).

    **[medium]**
11. **With an opaque buyer, protect the cash.** Use a lawyer in both jurisdictions, a strong contract, escrow, and a reference call with someone the buyer bought from before. Alex worried a secretive buyer might pay "only 20% at first" ([00:34:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=2070s), [00:34:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=2086s)). Expect a non-compete: his NDA bars him from the same space for a year ([00:39:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=2345s)). **[weak]**
12. **Due diligence** (do it as a buyer, expect it as a seller):
    - App Store Connect access, and checks that "the users aren't just bought" from cheap geos. For a weeks-old app this "legit took like a couple days" ([Caleb 00:49:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=2947s), [00:49:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=2954s)).
    - Market structure plus your own standardized test ads for the app you're looking at buying, then retention and marketing data ([Patrick, Social 00:07:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=421s)).
    - Which keywords the app ranks for and which it could still win ([Steve 00:42:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2547s)).
    - Larger deals take 4–5 months of negotiation and a ~100-page contract ([Alex 00:32:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=1933s)).

    **[strong]**
13. **Buy when distribution already works and monetization is broken, and you hold the fix.** Steve: "the best purchases I've made are the ones that have distribution but are either undermonetized or I can find a different distribution" ([00:42:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2535s)). Ryan bought Done (+5–6 apps), which was "quite undermonetized", and "within sort of 12 to 18 months we made our money back" ([00:06:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=373s), [00:06:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=387s)). Social buys apps "after they had a viral peak then they were declining" and grows them with ads and A/B tests ([00:09:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=593s), [00:13:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=810s)). **[strong]**
    - First moves after buying: keyword expansion in the title (see [aso-search](aso-search.md) rule 8) and a hard paywall/price rise. Steve's 2022 buy went from $1–2k/mo to $8–10k/mo with no new downloads ([00:18:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=1132s)).
14. **Buy vs build.** Build when you can find the keyword first (cheapest distribution; see [aso-search](aso-search.md)). Buy when you lack the time or domain to build, or when someone already proved the keyword: Steve skipped a keyword he knew, someone else built it to $500k/yr, and he bought it ([00:41:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2487s)). Ryan bought apps instead of spending the same money on ads, because the ads were "less reliable" and buying leaves you owning "the user base and the tech… that we can sell on afterwards" ([00:06:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=392s)). **[medium]**
15. **Debt financing** is possible for cash-flowing apps. Steve's example "to keep math easy": $10k/mo profit, $350k price, a 10-year loan at ~$5k/mo, "I'm technically profiting $5,000 a month" and pay back in ~3 years ([00:43:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2583s), [00:43:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2617s), [00:43:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2620s)). Seller financing also works ([00:45:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2701s)). It only works if the cash flow holds ("there's a bit of embedded risk") ([00:45:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2721s)). **[weak]** (illustrative, US loans)
16. **Flipping (build to sell).** "If you can get something to like even five to 10k MRR, you can sell that thing" ([Lots 00:18:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1122s)). Then repeat with the proceeds and skills. Talk about the app in public on X: both Lots' ([00:02:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=143s)) and Caleb's buyers came from tweets, Caleb's from an account with ~200 followers ([00:43:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=2630s), [00:44:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=2669s)). **[medium]**
17. **Sell rather than hold an app you've stopped working on.** Connor: "Like I'll do something with it later. Like no, you're not" ([00:44:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zpTXi8WxeM0&t=2658s)). Raphael sold Amanda AI because "we're probably not going to work on this anymore" ([00:28:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIgn-1kbzZw&t=1706s)). **[medium]**
18. **Hold** when growth is steep and you'd rather keep building. Jack Friks declined a ~$1M offer for PostBridge at ~$12k/mo while it grew 30–40% a month ([00:12:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=731s), [00:13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=780s)). He then concedes "the growth obviously slowed down and maybe it was silly to decline it" ([00:13:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=784s)). Note this is B2B SaaS. **[weak]**

## When the default flips
- **Strategic fit beats the multiple range.** A strategic with synergy pays the most ([Josh 00:35:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2151s)). Example: MyFitnessPal bought Cal AI's funnel, "they weren't buying tech" (Josh's read, *secondhand*; [00:39:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2388s)).
- **Paid-driven apps can sell well** to buyers whose edge is ads. Boost App Social ran on Meta ads and sold (see "Evidence against").
- **Retention can be missing if distribution is huge.** "60,000 downloads organically a month, they're just not hanging around… that's pretty interesting" ([Josh 00:41:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2460s)).
- **Sell very early** when you keep equity and the buyer adds what you lack. Caleb kept 30% plus an earn-out because the buyer brought developers, distribution and capital: "better off having 10% of a watermelon rather than… 80% of a grape" ([00:51:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=3069s)).
- **Hold** when the app is a cash-flow machine you enjoy, or when you're growing tens of percent a month (rule 18).

## Evidence against / where operators disagree
- **Sell fast vs scale first.** Lots and Caleb take quick, certain deals. Raphael regrets DBLI: "we probably should have scaled it more before selling it… now there's like a bunch of copycats, some of them are doing like 20, 30k a month" ([00:16:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIgn-1kbzZw&t=963s)). Josh splits the difference: sell before the peak, not at it.
- **Organic vs paid as a sale asset.** Steve and Josh discount paid-dependent apps. Yet Boost App Social was a Meta-ads business ([00:38:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=2280s)) that sold for seven figures, and Social buys apps specifically to scale them with its ad machine ([00:13:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=810s)). The buyer's skill decides which counts as an asset.
- **Price vs certainty.** Lots takes the third-highest price. Alex didn't rush and negotiated from strength. Coconote paid a bank to run a full process. Speed suits sub-$1M flips; process suits multi-million exits.
- **Hold vs sell at a high offer.** Jack kept PostBridge (now ~$20k MRR per the host, [00:13:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=829s)) and is unsure it was right. Caleb sold Runafy despite targeting $100k/mo, because of de-risking and capital for the next app ([00:52:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw5iIgO4PbY&t=3129s)).
- **Pattern worth knowing (2026):** gaming-origin money is buying subscription apps. Alex's buyer, Ryan's buyer and Josh himself (ex-Plarium, [00:04:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=282s)) all came from gaming.
- **Conflicts of interest:**
  - Josh is a buyer. His rules (organic, retention, sell before you need to) also lower what he pays for apps that lack them.
  - Steve runs an ASO agency/community, buys apps, and has a sponsor tool.
  - Lots runs a paid builder community and pitched his own app for sale on air ([00:17:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmIHgCRd04E&t=1032s)).
  - Superwall hosts the show.
  - All multiples are self-reported anecdotes, not a transaction dataset.

## Traps
- **Fake acquirers.** "Competitors of ours… would pretend to acquire us and then just basically take info from us" ([Dan Kwan 00:10:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2cDIFx4WWD0&t=653s)). Share data in stages.
- **M&A as a distraction.** "You're going to spend less time talking to customers… building… marketing" ([Coconote 00:36:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=2162s)).
- **Buying an app with no distribution.** "We paid $18,000 and it flopped" ([Steve 00:42:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=2526s)).
- **Being talked into selling.** A seller who was convinced bails "at the first hurdle" ([Josh 00:32:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1949s)).
- **Waiting until growth stalls.** If selling is your only option, "you don't have a lot of negotiating leverage" ([Josh 00:43:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=2622s)).
- **Sunk cost on an abandoned app** ([Connor 00:44:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zpTXi8WxeM0&t=2650s)). Connor tries to buy apps nobody has updated in two years, and owners refuse "because they have some sort of sunken cost" ([00:44:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zpTXi8WxeM0&t=2683s)) · [case file](../cases/payout.md)
- **Influencer-anchored apps without the influencer locked in.** Distribution leaves with the person (rule 9).
- **Weekly-plan, high-churn revenue** may be fine cash flow but is "not as good of a long-term strategy" ([Josh 00:31:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=1899s)). See [paywall-pricing](paywall-pricing.md).
