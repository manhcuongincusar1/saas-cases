# Onboarding teardown

**Use when** people install but don't reach or accept the paywall, or you are rebuilding onboarding.
**Decision card:** [onboarding-retention](../decisions/onboarding-retention.md). If this sheet and the card disagree, the card wins. Paywall side: [paywall-audit](paywall-audit.md).

> **Scope.** The evidence here is mostly about converting at the paywall, not about retention. The show is hosted by a paywall vendor, so "onboarding exists to sell the paywall" is the house view.

## Step 2 — Score the pitch: six ingredients (Welltory, 2026-08)

Welltory treats onboarding as a pitch with six ingredients: human problem → promise → key → mechanism → proof → FOMO — [00:18:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1118s). Treating onboarding as a pitch rather than a data form is **[strong]** in the card (rule 1). The six-part breakdown itself comes from one operator.

For each ingredient, check that it is **present** and **specific**.

**1. Human problem.** Ours, in the user's words: ______________
- [ ] Specific to a person and a moment of the day. Welltory's user once had a lot of energy and is now "on the couch at the end of the day doom scrolling" — [00:20:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1232s).
- [ ] Not skipped. Not spending enough time on it is "the biggest mistake". "That's not something that like AI can do for you … sit with it … for many months" — [00:20:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1251s).
- Welltory pitches the problem in the ad, not on screen 1 — [00:19:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1188s).

**2. Product promise.** Ours: ______________
- [ ] Bold on the first screen and deliberately incomplete, so the user stays curious. Welltory's: "Your Apple Watch knows what makes you tired and stressed. Hook up your data and we'll show you what's going on" — [00:21:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1277s).

**3. The key** — what we know that no other solution does: ______________
- [ ] A named idea that reframes the problem, made concrete with the user's own data. Welltory names "sedentary stress", then: "today, right now, you already have 192 minutes of it" — [00:21:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1300s), [00:21:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1318s).

**4. Mechanism** — how the product hands them the key: ______________
- [ ] Shown rather than promised. Welltory shows on the timeline of your day when stress happens and when you relieved it: "right now you're only relieving like 20%" — [00:22:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1340s).

**5. Proof.** Our strongest: ______________
- [ ] Something personal to this user. "The most powerful proof that we use actually is that we are showing you your own data" — [00:23:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1391s).
- [ ] Supporting proof spread through the flow: reviews, user counts, journal publications, university partners — [00:23:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1412s).

**6. FOMO** — what they lose today by leaving: ______________
- [ ] A real consequence today, not a countdown timer. Welltory: "you won't know when the bad stress is happening, it'll keep draining you, and you'll feel just as tired tonight" — [00:23:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1430s). This was the step where he "kept failing spectacularly" — [00:19:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1165s).
- **Condition:** a benefits ending "worked worse" for Welltory — [00:35:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2127s). For apps with a natural trigger (Spotify, Uber), benefits sell fine — [00:37:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2273s). **[weak]**
- **Disagreement:** Clear 30 and PushScroll sell the dream outcome instead (card, "Fear vs aspiration").

**If an ingredient is missing, rebuild the story arc before polishing copy.** Welltory: "I used to get really fixated on getting like the copy perfect, but it didn't work until … I had like the whole story arc" — [00:25:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1559s).

## Step 3 — Structural checks

- [ ] **Users know what the app does before you ask them questions.** Sway kept getting "what does this app even do?" until explainer screens came first — [00:23:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=1435s).
- [ ] **Questions are tap-to-answer and easy.** Sway: avoid questions that are "hard for you to come up with an answer for" — [00:24:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=1495s). Phoenix Do: no "more than like five words on each screen" — [00:13:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7eaHcznLZU&t=839s).
- [ ] **Answers or data are echoed back to personalize the flow.** Phoenix Do: "a couple questions … make that experience feel personal doubled our conversion" — [00:12:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D7eaHcznLZU&t=776s). Clear 30 asks for the user's name, then uses it in the next question — [00:10:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYcU4_HQdIk&t=659s).
- [ ] **If one permission unlocks personalization, it is the first ask, framed as curiosity. [weak]**
  - Welltory's first button is "sync my health data", and over 90% sync — [00:27:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1649s).
  - Its founder admits "probably we haven't tested it enough", though every prelude he tried "didn't do as well" — [00:27:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1642s).
- [ ] **Trust screens come right before any scary permission** (screen time, health). PushScroll: the screen-time permission is "one big drop off", which "can be good for conversion but bad for paywall rate" — [00:44:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdudtXwEsOQ&t=2666s).
- [ ] **A taste of the aha moment comes before the paywall only if it costs zero effort.**
  - PushScroll lets users try push-up detection once — [00:49:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdudtXwEsOQ&t=2952s).
  - Sway's photo uploads before the paywall made users leave and "never [come] back" — [00:21:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=1301s).
  - Gating right after onboarding beat explore-first by 50% at GlamUp — [00:44:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bf12MNPO-Js&t=2682s).
- [ ] **Micro-commitments come before the ask.** PushScroll asks when you want to work out. An earlier version had users sign their name under "from this day onward, I will…". Its claim that users are "more likely to convert on the paywall" is *heuristic*, not measured — [00:49:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdudtXwEsOQ&t=2976s), [00:49:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdudtXwEsOQ&t=2986s).
- [ ] **Length is chosen for the audience.**
  - Longer usually wins when the extra screens personalize or prove. Coconote doubled its onboarding to ~13 screens and got +16% initial trial conversion — [00:29:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=1761s), [00:29:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=1783s).
  - Keep it short for under-25s. Pep AI branches by age — [00:28:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOpYezjqU9c&t=1738s).
  - PushScroll is testing a shorter version because "the payroll [paywall] rate isn't the best" — [00:50:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdudtXwEsOQ&t=3002s).

## Step 4 — Rebuild queue

**Make big moves, not one-screen tweaks.** Coconote's one-or-two-screens-at-a-time testing gave "a lot of small wins, but difficult to make big progress". Rebuilding the whole flow gave +16% — [00:28:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=1738s). Fix missing ingredients before polishing ones that are already present.

Measure **install → paywall view** and **paywall conversion** together. A longer or permission-heavy flow can raise one and lower the other.
