# Yannis — reposter model: own the creative centrally, validate a converting format before building the app

## Idea — how they found it
- Post-COVID, moved from B2B ML research to consumer; started by posting manually on his own account "maybe 50 to 100 times in French" to feel every friction step: views ≠ followers ≠ conversion ≠ traffic — [02:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=177s).
- Inspiration for multi-account scaling: the "tabs chocolate guy" (e-commerce) — [04:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=264s). First fleet built for a friend's dating app; he programmatically generated viral text conversations on a Tinder/iMessage clone and screen-recorded them — [04:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=287s), [06:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=381s).
- **Reverse the order**: pick a niche → find formats that are viral AND move App Store ranks → get one account viral with that format a few times → then build. "I wouldn't build my app without trying to go viral first with a high converting format" — [54:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=3240s). He is doing this now for a claims/settlement app — [13:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=830s).

## Product — what they built and the key product choices
- Not a single product case; the "product" is the content system. Product lesson he draws: in a niche, place the app's mechanic inside native value content (e.g., face-exercise app shows exercises, then its evaluation/score screen as the payoff) — [51:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=3084s).

## Marketing — channel, formats, creator/ads machine
- **Reposter vs ambassador**: reposters only warm up accounts and post; the team keeps creative control ("something I've never liked is externalizing the creative process") — [04:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=287s). Ambassadors = delegated creativity, which "removes some of the predictability" — [22:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1368s); reposter accounts can be spun up fast with "approximately the same success rate every time" — [23:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1387s); an ambassador's single account can be shadowbanned — [23:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1411s).
- If you do use ambassadors: strict brief, "80% of the post you have to respect a structure, 20% you can explore" — [24:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1490s). "We're not artists... We have a recipe. We're trying to convert some people" — [26:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1594s).
- **Research**: separate FORMAT (transferable, e.g. hook→demo) from CONTENT (domain-specific, e.g. red-background finance news + screenshot overlay) — [29:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1764s). A "gem" = same shot-by-shot structure viral across ~5–20 posts/accounts — [28:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1697s). Only study outliers: posts >200× the median views of their own account, which removes the "famous face" effect — [49:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2999s).
- **Scalability filter**: reject skit/street-interview formats with many variables (Cheater Buster family) — [32:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1944s); prefer slideshows and formats AI or humans can mass-produce, differentiated by small variations around one premise.
- **Sounds**: "TikTok is sounds-based first" — [40:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2457s); curate sounds per angle — [40:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2442s); ~20–30% of good sounds are years old, sped up/remixed — [41:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2517s).
- **Duration by niche**: 7–9 s quick; 9–15 s when the app needs explaining — [43:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2615s); ~30 s full skit (prank apps) — [43:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2631s); health/finance audiences expect longer content; a short finance bait-and-switch reaches the wrong people — [42:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2556s).
- **Views ≠ downloads (scratch-map test)**: same creative, only the text overlay changed. "Post breakup activity" → "millions of views" but zero success — [37:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2248s), [39:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2348s); "procrastinating for my exams" → app jumped in the App Store "in several countries" — [38:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2293s). His guess: immediacy of need + US exam timing — [38:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2308s).
- **Comment bait seeding**: a finance post ("I want to have 10k saved by May, give me your most unhinged...") with 10M views / 1M likes / 5.4k comments never shows an app — [11:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=697s), [12:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=733s); after a 10M-view post, the following posts get a "ripple effect", so they can be solution-oriented and convert — [13:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=794s).
- **Attribution via App Store ranks**: correlate viral-post dates with chart moves. A 16M-view post (and 7M) for one app never got it above ~#170 — [48:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2925s), [48:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2935s); others do 1M views and jump top 100 — [49:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2941s); apps with 100k–200k-view outliers can jump more than prank apps with huge views — [46:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2760s). Organic wins appear in several English-speaking countries at once, often not first in the US; paid does not do that — [47:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=2869s).
- **Channel choice**: organic suits impulse installs, novelty, low budget — [21:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1291s); with budget, run some paid ads first to check the product converts — [21:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1271s); "everything where you remove dollars" you pay with your own time — [20:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=1247s).
- **Founder posts first**: "you cannot manage a team in this realm without going through the motions" — [54:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=3297s), [55:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=3326s).

## Lessons → decisions
- Validate a converting format on one account before building; pick the niche from formats that already move App Store ranks → [choose-idea](../decisions/choose-idea.md), [build-or-kill](../decisions/build-or-kill.md)
- Reposters (central creative, cheap accounts) vs ambassadors (delegated creative): choose by whether you can run creative research yourself → [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)
- Judge content by rank/download movement, not views; intent and timing of the text overlay decide conversion → [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md), [ugc-creators](../decisions/ugc-creators.md)
- Organic for impulse/novelty/low-ticket and low budget; with budget, test conversion with paid first → [choose-channel](../decisions/choose-channel.md), [paid-ads](../decisions/paid-ads.md)

## Caveats
- Numbers are approximate and blend own apps with client work; he says clients don't share hybrid results, so download attribution is uncertain — [57:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=3447s).
- Transcript contradicts itself on monthly views: intro clip says "10 to 15 million views per month" [00:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=5s), later "10 to 50 million" [57:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eh1SDHX84vQ&t=3427s).
- Much of the research demo runs on SpyTok, the episode's paid sponsor; he shows an unreleased SpyTok feature — treat tool endorsements as possibly conflicted. He also sells this as a service to clients. Superwall hosts.
- Multi-account reposter farms are platform arbitrage; other operators (Sunflower) expect them to be shut down. Dated May 2026.
